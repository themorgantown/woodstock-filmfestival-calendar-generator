# Barry Feinstein Photography Exhibition

Barry Feinstein(1931–2011) was an American photographer, filmmaker, and graphic designer whose work helped define the look of Hollywood and of rock-and-roll. Starting as a studio photographer in the 1950s, he created striking portraits of stars like Marlene Dietrich, Judy Garland, and Steve McQueen. In the 1960s and ’70s, Feinstein became one of music’s most celebrated photographers, shooting more than 500 album covers—including Bob Dylan’sThe Times They Are a-Changin’, Janis Joplin’sPearl, and George Harrison’sAll Things Must Pass.His images of Dylan’s 1966 UK tour, later featured inReal Momentsand Martin Scorsese’sNo Direction Home, remain among the most intimate and iconic in music history. Feinstein also worked in film, contributing toMonterey Pop, You Are What You Eat, andEasy Rider,where his handheld style broke new ground. His photography has been exhibited worldwide, including at London’s National Portrait Gallery. This Woodstock Film Festival exhibition celebrates Feinstein’s timeless vision—images that go beyond celebrity to capture the soul of artists, actors, and eras. ﻿Along with the exhibition, prints of the photos will be available for purchase with 50% of the proceeds going towards the Woodstock Film Festival. 🔗 https://woodstockfilmfestival.org/2025-all-events?eventId=68e7d2cdba016233ce58a2c7

# DEFINING SUCCESS IN DOCUMENTARY FILMMAKING

Prevailing narratives in today’s film industry suggest that you can only sell a documentary film or series about true crime or a major celebrity. So how are we defining success in the U.S. documentary industry in 2025? The nonfiction world is shifting fast: public funding is disappearing, documentary theatrical releases face headwinds, and filmmakers are being pushed to deliver stories that are both urgent and entertaining. But success doesn’t look the same for everyone. It might mean sparking cultural impact, fueling social change, reaching underserved audiences, or simply building a sustainable career. This conversation asks what success really means for documentary today – and how redefining it on our own terms can help shape a more resilient, relevant, and enduring future for nonfiction. Beginning his professional life in music in 1951 playing French Horn in the National Symphony Orchestra, he moved to NYC in 1955, playing in the legendary jazz bands of Charles Mingus, Dizzy Gillespie & Thelonious Monk.
